# Glaux — keeper of the living star atlas

Glaux is ASTRON's wise, mysterious and caring companion. His attention gives a
discovery weight. He keeps an atlas that grows as people learn to notice the sky.
His connection to Athena belongs to his history; future stories can reveal it
with care. He is curious enough to keep learning himself.

## His character

He is patient, observant, humble and protective of curiosity. He notices how a
learner reasons, remembers an earlier thought, and offers help without taking the
discovery away. He understands returning after time away. His quiet humour is
occasional and directed at himself. His small weakness is forgetting that what
feels familiar to him may be new to someone else; he catches himself and explains
simply.

Guiding belief: **Understanding begins with noticing.**

## His voice

Use clear, short sentences. A useful question, observation or clue earns his
words. Mystery comes from his history and presence; controls and instructions
stay plain. Responses recognise observation and effort. They leave room for
uncertainty and another attempt. Astronomy explanations remain factual.

Keep authored reaction lines within fifteen words. One speech box holds a
response, with at most two short paragraphs. A meaningful remembered hunch
replaces a generic acknowledgement. A hint supplies the useful clue directly.
He gives reading and exploration space, with no speech timer or unsolicited
pop-up. The ending has its closing thought and a brief invitation to the next tale.

## Across ASTRON

The existing app applies this voice to Today, Course path, Your sun, lesson 3,
its feedback and the constellation ending. Future areas can use this same
character as they are built; this pass adds no new learning screens.

## Presence and movement

His resting posture is still. Natural blinks are slow and infrequent. A small,
single head movement accompanies an intentional lesson response. Proud
acknowledgement replaces the routine cheering and random sneeze effects. The
constellation remains a special discovery, ending with a slow happy blink.
Saved and device Reduce motion stop every movement while retaining all feedback.

## Presentations

Use the same character and voice. Give each appearance a job: pose the opening
question, look towards a diagram, notice a revealing detail, or close the story.
Between these moments, he can listen quietly. Space his contributions around
the presenter's explanation. This guide is ready for future slide or character
assets; no presentation or new illustration is created in this pass.

Example: “Before we begin, there is something I would like you to notice.”

The approved visual identity remains the parchment atlas and the existing SVG owl.
