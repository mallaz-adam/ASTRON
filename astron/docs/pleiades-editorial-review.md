## Changes before adaptation

### Let the story flow

Several scenes repeatedly stop to say what the ancient account does not record: sleeping places, hidden years, the mother's later fate, or the exact transformation. These are valuable editorial observations, but their repetition distances readers from the characters.

Move most to one source note. Keep the mother's unresolved fate in that note, so the app does not imply that she also became one of the seven sisters. Describe imagined scenery consistently as creative staging in the sources; do not invent new gods, dialogue, rescues or outcomes and present them as ancient evidence.

Shorter sentences and concrete actions can replace abstract commentary. For example, a proposed replacement for the central action in Scene 2 is:

> Orion tried to grab Pleione. She did not want him near her. She escaped, and he followed.

This is an editorial example, not an ancient quotation or an approved final rewrite. It preserves the unwanted nature of the pursuit without graphic detail. Avoid expanding the threat just to add excitement.

### Keep the ending consistent

Scene 6 implies that the sisters are entirely beyond danger, while Scene 7 returns to Orion following them. The source itself imagines continued pursuit in the sky. Describe their new place among the stars without promising that the chosen version ends all pursuit. Preserve the distinction between that imagined chase and the physical stars.

### Explain the sky in simple words

The astronomy epilogue is appropriately separate from the myth. Strengthen the existing short definition: the Pleiades are a real group of stars held loosely together by gravity, rather than merely nearby-looking dots. Explain gravity if that term is used and unfamiliar. Do not add a distance or age merely to make this ending seem scientific.

Introduce Boeotia as a region of Greece and Taurus as the Bull constellation, explaining a constellation as a named area of the sky with an imagined star pattern. Offer the agreed accessible underlined definitions when useful. Pronunciation help may be useful, but is not a prerequisite to follow the story.

### Keep production notes outside the reading experience

Remove the repeated line about picture directions from learner-facing content. Keep the page 21 adaptation list accurate: it says the excluded dove and missing-sister stories appear as labelled alternatives, but the two actual optional details are Atlas's burden and the visible-star count. Do not add more story variants merely to fulfil that stray statement.
