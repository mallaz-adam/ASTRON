# Unit 4 review: Galaxies and the universe

Reviewed 2 November 2026. Source: the owner's 13-page
ASTRON_Chapter1_Unit4_Galaxies_and_the_Universe_REVIEW.pdf in Downloads.
Read all pages, inspected rendered pages, extracted the embedded source links,
and checked the principal science and five proposed image-source pages online.
This is an editorial review; the original PDF and application are unchanged.

## Recommendation

Use this manuscript as the basis for Unit 4, with the small editorial and visual
changes below approved before implementation. It does not need a wholesale
rewrite or a longer lesson simply to add screens. Preserve the introduction,
ten learner-paced teaching parts, separate recap, two optional ungraded
practices, glossary, limited Glaux presence and clear source/type captions.

The substantive new learning is other galaxies, different appearances, the
universe, and interpreting our limited view. Recall Unit 3 briefly, then move
forward. The existing Unit 3 already introduces galaxies, gravity and the
photograph/reconstruction distinction.

## What works

- A connected move from our known home to other galaxies and the universe.
- Plain explanations of gas, dust, gravity and processed observations.
- No unnecessary distance numbers, expansion history or premature Big Bang lesson.
- Honest treatment of photographs, diagrams and scientific reconstructions.
- Original explanatory models are explicitly schematic, and static alternatives
  retain the meaning of the optional controls.
- Practices are ungraded with specific explanations and retry support.
- Source pages, credits, licences and accessible descriptions are provided.

## Changes recommended before implementation

### 1. Keep the object in the text and picture consistent

Pages 3 and 5: Step 5 introduces Andromeda, but V2 assigns M51 to Steps 4–5.
Page 6 assigns a deep field to V4, while Practice 2 describes Andromeda.
These are individually valid examples, but changing objects without a clear
transition could confuse a beginner.

Proposed assignment:

| Teaching part | Picture and purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction / Step 1 | Compact original relationship diagram; recall the known home. |
| Steps 2–3 | One labelled galaxy model, briefly recalling material and gravity. |
| Step 4 | Hubble Ultra Deep Field: many other galaxies in one small observed field. |
| Step 5 | The actual Andromeda wide-field observation, with this object's name explicit. |
| Step 6 | M51 and IC 2006 for appearance comparison, with equal panel size explicitly unrelated to real size. |
| Step 7 | Reuse an earlier observed image beside its matching explanatory diagram. |
| Step 8 | Reuse the already credited Gaia-informed Milky Way reconstruction from Unit 3. |
| Steps 9–10 | Relationship map and a restrained explanatory card about the limits of observation. |

Do not label a ground-based DSS2 Andromeda observation as a Hubble exposure just
because ESA/Hubble hosts its source page. Do not download the very large original
files for a phone lesson: use appropriately sized local assets, document the
conversion and test their offline cache cost. Preserve required credit/licence
information; keep the reading layout compact without hiding required notices.

### 2. Let the second practice build beyond Unit 3

Pages 4, 6–8: Practice 2 asks for image types, while V4's always-visible captions
already name those types. Unit 3 also teaches photograph versus reconstruction.
Keep truthful captions visible; make the practice about what can be concluded.

Proposed practice theme: **What can these pictures tell us?**

- Compare M51 and IC 2006: differing appearances are supported, but equal panel
  dimensions do not establish equal galaxy sizes.
- Compare the observed image and the original diagram: the diagram explains
  selected features, not a separate telescope measurement.
- Consider the Gaia picture: it is an evidence-guided outside view, not proof
  that a camera photographed the entire Milky Way from outside.

Write the final prompts and choices for owner review. Keep this practice
optional, ungraded and keyboard/touch accessible; explain each attempt. No new
assessment records, quest rewards or completion gates belong to this practice.

### 3. Give the observable-universe idea a simple reason

Page 3, Step 10 currently gives a name and warns against a wall, but does not
explain why observations have a reach. Add a short bridge without numerical
cosmology or a second lesson on light travel.

Suggested addition: "Light needs time to travel. Some regions are too far away
for their light to have reached us."

Then retain the distinction between observational reach and the whole universe.
Do not equate the observable universe with what today's telescope happens to
resolve, or imply that better equipment removes the light-travel limit. Do not
turn the fading storyboard into a measured map of what lies beyond our view.

### 4. Tighten the recap

Page 4: the last item joins the picture-type distinction to "The universe
includes them all." That pronoun mixes images with the astronomical objects
being introduced. Give the universe and image-reading ideas clear wording.

Suggested three-item recap:

1. Our solar system lies in the Milky Way, one of many galaxies.
2. The universe includes all space and everything in it.
3. Telescope images, diagrams and reconstructions provide different kinds of information.

Gravity is the binding relationship; "partly by gravity" in the original second
recap is less clear than the main explanation. The list of visible material need
not claim to inventory everything in a galaxy or introduce dark matter now.

### 5. Rebuild the sketches as readable teaching visuals

The source PDF explicitly calls its sketches placeholders. Its five image
candidates are linked, not embedded photographs. It is not a finished art pack.

On page 5, the nested boxes crowd/overlap some of their upper-right labels.
Reserve a separate label area for each level, including at 390 pixels, and
explain that the boxes represent membership, not walls or relative dimensions.
On page 3, the recap heading is stranded before its items on page 4; this is a
PDF layout issue, not a proposed extra learner screen.

Keep editorial instructions such as how a drawing should be made out of the
learner's narration. For Step 10, a clear learner-facing sentence about the
observational limit is better than a direction to an illustrator.

Text extraction replaced some bullet glyphs even though the rendered PDF bullets
look correct. Author clean JSON from reviewed text rather than copying those
replacement characters into the app. Preserve source links independently of
their short PDF display labels.

  beginner observable-universe definition.
- [AMNH Observable Universe](https://www.amnh.org/exhibitions/permanent/scales-of-the-universe/observable)

  supports the light-travel bridge. Detailed timing remains for Unit 6.

This is a targeted verification of the central claims and proposed media, not
an assertion that the manuscript's entire confidence table was independently
audited line by line or that downloaded image renditions have been inspected.
