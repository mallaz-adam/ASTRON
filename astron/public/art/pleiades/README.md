# Pleiades artwork

Sources and reuse statements checked 3 November 2026. Files are served locally
and precached by the production PWA. Essential pictures never use remote embeds.

## Historical painting

- `vedder-640.webp` (640 x 419) and `vedder-1280.webp` (1280 x 838).
- Elihu Vedder, *The Pleiades*, 1885, oil on canvas.
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art, Gift of George A. Hearn, 1910 (10.64.13).
- [Exact museum object](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/13070).
- The museum marks this object Public Domain; its API returned
  `isPublicDomain: true` for object 13070.
- [Open Access policy](https://www.metmuseum.org/about-the-met/policies-and-documents/open-access).
- Original downloaded from the museum API's `primaryImage` for that object.
  Preparation: resized and WebP-compressed only, with no repainting or AI edit.
- This nineteenth-century interpretation includes a broken light-thread and a
  missing-star motif. It is neither ancient Greek evidence nor a literal image
  of the selected Hyginus sequence. The cover says so.

## Modern atlas illustrations

`scene-1.svg` through `scene-7.svg` are original code-drawn ASTRON illustrations.
The invented landscapes, clothing, poses and seasonal panels are modern visual
storytelling. They are not archaeological reconstructions, historical paintings,
documented routes or records of the events. Scene 5 is mythic symbolism; Scene 6
does not illustrate physical star formation. Scene 7 explicitly uses symbolic
placement and is not a sky-finding chart.

`cluster-pattern.svg` is an original explanatory diagram. Its points illustrate
the idea of a group containing bright and faint stars. They are not measured
Pleiades positions and do not label a physical star. The control calls it a
**Cluster diagram**, and its caption/alternative description explain that limit.

All eight SVGs have a 640 x 420 viewBox, static shapes, gradients and small
repeating hatching/grain patterns. There are no timers, external fonts, image
embeds, filters or animation loops. The seven sisters are not a claim that every
observer sees seven physical stars.

No APOD photograph, unlicensed third-party illustration or Bayer source scan is
included. The APOD page remains a source link only.
